The Arthur Curtis loop

Goal, Map, Fund, Report.

Four stages that turn a training budget into a workforce plan you can stand behind in front of the board.

01

Goal

Define the people outcomes the business actually needs — not a catalogue of courses. Close a gap, upskill a team, recruit, or get the most from funding.

02

Map

Match roles to the right standards and providers, and map a path for every learner — apprenticeships first, short courses where they fit better.

03

Fund

Engineer the spend so the levy covers it, month by month, before it expires — co-investment, transfers and incentives all accounted for.

04

Report

Prove impact with reporting leadership can trust: what was spent, what it bought, and what changed — traceable to the source.

Why tooling-led

We don't just advise. We build the software.

Every other levy consultancy hands you a slide deck. We hand you working tools — the same calculator, matcher and planners we use internally. The site demonstrates the capability instead of describing it.

That matters because it forces honesty. When a recommendation has to survive a live calculator, there's nowhere to hide a fudged number. Every figure we show is traceable to gov.uk or a dated funding rulebook — bands, co-investment rates, age gates, defunding dates. No fabricated multipliers, no rounded-up ROI.

The result is the one thing that actually moves a budget decision: a number a CFO would stake a plan on without checking it elsewhere.

Principles

The rules we hold ourselves to.

  • ✓Answer-first. If you just want to know what your levy can fund, you get a number in seconds — before any conversation.
  • ✓Traceable, not plausible. Every lever we recommend is verified against the funding rules before it reaches you.
  • ✓Funded before expiry. As of August 2026 unspent levy is gone in 12 months, not 24. We plan spend against that tighter clock, month by month.
  • ✓Independent. We don't sell you courses. We're not a training provider, so the plan is built around your goal, not our catalogue.
  • ✓Provable impact. If we can't show what changed, we haven't finished.
