The problem

The money’s already gone. The question is whether it works for you.

The levy leaves your payroll every month at 0.5% of your pay bill, above a £15,000 allowance. Then a clock starts — tightened to just 12 months from August 2026 (down from 24) — and anything you don’t spend simply expires back to the Treasury.

Most employers under-use it, not because they don’t care, but because the rules are genuinely hard: co-investment, transfers, age gates, band caps, defunding dates. We turn that complexity into a plan you can act on — and a number you can defend.

Questions

The things employers actually ask.

Do we have to use apprenticeships? +
No. Apprenticeships are usually the most funding-efficient route, so we start there — but the plan is built around your goal. Where a short course or CPD fits better, we say so.
What if we’re not a levy payer? +
You’re still covered. For non-levy employers the government pays the large majority of training costs through co-investment — we model exactly what that means for your headcount and budget so there are no surprises.
Will our levy really expire? +
Yes — and faster than before. As of August 2026 new funds expire just 12 months after they enter your account, oldest first (funds banked before then keep the old 24-month clock). That tighter clock is the whole reason a plan matters.
How is this different from a training provider? +
A provider’s advice points at the courses they deliver. We’re independent — we don’t sell training — so the plan is built around your goal, and every figure is traceable to the funding rules, not a catalogue.
Do you deliver the training yourselves? +
No. We plan, fund and prove it — then help you choose and manage the right providers. That independence is the point.
