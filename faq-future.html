The levy, in plain terms
+What is the apprenticeship levy?
A payroll charge of 0.5% of your annual pay bill, collected through PAYE. Every employer gets a £15,000 allowance, so in practice only employers with a pay bill above £3 million actually pay it. What you pay goes into a digital account to spend on apprenticeship training.
gov.uk — Pay the levy →verified 26 Aug 2026
+Do we even pay the levy?
Only if your annual pay bill is over £3 million. Below that you're a non-levy employer — you don't pay in, but the government still funds most of your apprenticeship training and you contribute a small share (see below).
gov.uk — Pay the levy →verified 26 Aug 2026
+What changed in August 2026?
Three things worth knowing: the 10% government top-up was removed on new funds; unused funds now expire after 12 months (down from 24); and training for 16–24-year-olds is now 100% government-funded. We model the current rules and flag every change.
gov.uk — Technical funding guide (from Aug 2026) →verified 26 Aug 2026
+Does unused levy expire?
Yes. Funds expire 12 months after they enter your apprenticeship service account, spent oldest-first. That's for funds entering from August 2026 — anything banked before then keeps the old 24-month clock. It's why planning matters: unspent levy is money that walks out the door.
gov.uk — Technical funding guide →verified 26 Aug 2026
Funding — who pays for what
+Is there still a 10% top-up on our levy?
No. The 10% government top-up was removed for funds entering accounts from 1 August 2026. What you declare is what reaches your account — nothing is added on top for new funds.
gov.uk — Technical funding guide →verified 26 Aug 2026
+We're not a levy payer — what does it cost us?
For apprentices aged 16–24, training is 100% government-funded — you pay nothing toward it. For those aged 25 and over, you co-invest 5% and the government pays 95%.
gov.uk — Technical funding guide →verified 26 Aug 2026
+What happens if our levy funds run out?
If you're a levy payer and your account is empty, apprentices aged 16–24 stay 100% funded. For those aged 25+, you co-invest 25% and the government pays 75% on continued training.
gov.uk — Technical funding guide →verified 26 Aug 2026
+Can we give our levy to another employer?
Yes — you can transfer up to 50% of your annual funds to other employers (for example, in your supply chain), covering their apprenticeship training up to the funding band. It's a way to put levy to work rather than letting it expire.
gov.uk — Transferring your levy →verified 26 Aug 2026
Money in — incentives & savings
+Is there any money for taking on an apprentice?
Yes — a £1,000 payment to support the apprentice in the workplace, if at the start of training they're 16–18, or 19–24 with an EHC plan or care-experienced. It's paid in two £500 instalments and is separate from the training funding.
gov.uk — Get funding →verified 26 Aug 2026
+What's the new £2,000 hiring payment?
From 1 October 2026, a hiring incentive of up to £2,000 for non-levy employers taking on a new apprentice aged 16–24. It's a distinct, newer scheme — we'll flag which one applies to your situation.
gov.uk — Get funding →verified 26 Aug 2026
+Do we save on National Insurance?
Yes. For apprentices under 25 on an approved apprenticeship, employers pay 0% secondary National Insurance on earnings up to the Apprentice Upper Secondary Threshold of £50,270 a year. Above that, the normal employer rate resumes.
gov.uk — Rates & thresholds 2026–27 →verified 26 Aug 2026
+Do apprentices have to be young or new hires?
No. Apprenticeships fund new and existing staff, at any age — they're a way to develop your current team, not just entry-level recruits. How much the government funds depends on the apprentice's age and prior learning, not on whether they're new.
gov.uk — Technical funding guide →verified 26 Aug 2026
Getting started
+How much time out for training is required?
An apprentice must spend an average of at least 6 hours a week on off-the-job training across the practical period — learning that's part of their working hours, not evenings and weekends. It's the biggest real cost of an apprenticeship, and we cost it honestly.
gov.uk — Apprenticeship funding rules →verified 26 Aug 2026
+How long does an apprenticeship take?
At least 8 months, but most run 12–48 months depending on the standard and level. Each standard has a typical duration — and in the 2-5 Model, two apprenticeships bookend a five-year climb from junior to senior.
gov.uk — Apprenticeship funding rules →verified 26 Aug 2026
+How do we know a course is actually funded?
Every apprenticeship standard has a funding band (the maximum the government will pay) and a status — approved, or being defunded. Our tools check the standard, its band, duration and any age catches for you, so you're not planning against a course that's about to lose funding.
gov.uk — Funding bands →verified 26 Aug 2026
