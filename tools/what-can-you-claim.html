Hiring a care leaver — what it means +
A care leaver is someone who's been in local-authority care (an 'eligible', 'relevant' or 'former relevant' child). If they're under 25 at the start, they receive a £3,000 bursary (paid in three instalments over the first year, and it's tax- and NI-free for them) — and as the employer you may get the £1,000 incentive, extended eligibility to 100% funded training, and the NI relief. Beyond the money: care-experienced young people are one of the most under-represented groups in work, so it's a genuine social-value hire. Confirm status is evidenced by the local authority. gov.uk guidance →
Apprentices with an EHC plan +
An Education, Health and Care (EHC) plan is a legal document for a young person with special educational needs or a disability. A 19–24 apprentice with an EHCP unlocks the same extended eligibility as a care leaver: the £1,000 incentive, small-employer 100% funding, and (for Level 7) funding up to age 24. Learning Support funding of £150/month is also available where reasonable adjustments are needed to complete the training. gov.uk guidance →
Small employer (under 50 staff) +
If you employ fewer than 50 people, the government fully funds the training (100% up to the band maximum) for a 16–18 apprentice, or a 19–24 care leaver / EHCP apprentice. For everyone else, non-levy employers co-invest 5% and the government pays 95%. gov.uk funding rules →
